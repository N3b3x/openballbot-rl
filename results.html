Results at a glance

Five charts, one controller

The whole corrected-engine study on one screen: how goal success rose recipe by recipe, where the learned authority should sit, what still makes the robot fall, when seeing the terrain matters, and what carries over to a robot it never trained on. Each chart links to the chapter of the generalist study that holds the full tables and the caveats.

SIMULATION ONLY · MuJoCo-Warp float32 GPU, corrected engine (S69 onward)

TL;DR
  • The recommended controller is an LQR on the derived planar model plus a learned residual with authority 0.25, on a robot fitted with three passive ball arrestors. It tracks speed closely (0.85 to 1.00 of the command, error at most 0.054 m/s, no falls) and reaches a goal across ten terrain kinds 0.73 to 0.74 of the time undisturbed and 0.45 to 0.46 with pushes and pulls, on the three recommended checkpoints (g26, corrected contact model, S99 close-out; the same recipe on the old model: 0.90 to 1.01, 0.71 to 0.76 and 0.42 to 0.44).
  • The arrestors, not more training, were the step change: without them the same recipe reaches 0.58 to 0.60 and 0.29 to 0.30. In 95 percent of the earlier falls the body derailed off the ball at about 15 deg of tilt (S79).
  • What remains: obstacles larger than about a quarter of the ball radius fail at any speed, a traction limit; where they can be avoided, a forward time-of-flight sensor plus a planner takes goal success from 0.09 (blind) to 0.85, against 0.91 with a perfect map.

This page is a summary and repeats nothing else: the per-terrain tables, the null results (wider randomisation, disturbance-focused variants, a depth sensor for the balance policy, training with the planner in the loop) and the corrections are in the generalist study; the clips behind these numbers are on the videos page. Colours keep one meaning across the site: teal is the LQR plus learned residual, violet is pure learning, green is success, amber is a limit and red is a fall. Every value is a share of simulated worlds; the sample sizes above say how far to trust a difference.
