## Fact sheet (corrected engine, MuJoCo-Warp float32 GPU, 4096 worlds, 100 Hz)
Recommended controller: LQR on the derived planar model (per-design gains, Q = diag(1e-6, 50, 1, 1), R = 1, velocity reference
governor a_max 0.5 m/s^2, velocity integral gain 1, yaw loop) plus a PPO residual with authority 0.25 (applied action =
clip(a_LQR + 0.25 a_policy)), on a robot fitted with three Rezero-style ball arrestors (passive 8 mm rollers at 100 deg polar
angle, touching the ball only at the loaded seat). Trained on 11 robot designs, domain randomisation scale 1.75 (mass, friction,
torque, damping, latency, noise), pushes 5 to 20 N for 50 ms, pulls 3 to 12 N for 0.5 to 2 s, 200M steps.
- Flat tracking: achieved 0.99 to 1.01 of the commanded speed, error at most 0.04 to 0.06 m/s up to 0.9 m/s (3 seeds).
- Goal task (1.1 m goal, terrain tiles, 11 designs): success 0.71 to 0.76 undisturbed, 0.42 to 0.44 with pushes and pulls (3 seeds).
  Without arrestors: 0.58 to 0.60 and 0.29 to 0.30. Pure learning: 0.44 to 0.51 and 0.26 to 0.27, tracking only 0.54 to 0.66.
- Why it fell before the arrestors: in 95 percent of falls the body derailed off the ball at about 15 deg tilt; torque saturation under 1 percent (S79).
- The authority dial (all with arrestors): 0.15 -> tracking 1.01, robust 0.35; 0.25 -> 1.00, 0.43; 0.35 -> 0.95, 0.45; 0.5 -> 0.85, 0.47;
  pure learning -> 0.74, 0.50 (S82, S83).
- Unseen robot designs (S85-R): LQR-based controllers keep 0.61 to 0.71 goal success on a 1.5x robot; pure learning collapses to 0.09 to 0.15;
  a tenth-torque design is motor limited for every controller (at most 0.30).
- Terrain (S87, S88): obstacle falls depend on size, not speed. Traction limit h_max = r (1 - cos(arctan mu)), about 0.29 r: 2.6 cm for the
  9 cm ball, 4.0 cm for 13.5 cm. Steps and bumps above it fail 90 to 98 percent at any speed; the 13.5 cm ball crosses more. A perfect
  height map given to the balance policy, or a depth sensor, does not reduce those falls.
- Navigation around unclimbable blocks (S89, S90): blind 0.09 success / 0.91 falls; perfect map 0.91 / 0.09; forward ToF 18x14 on the head
  0.85 (jerky planner) or 0.78 with the smooth follower and the sensor aimed at the planner target (heading-rate RMS 1.8 against 14 rad/s);
  mount and pitch matter more than resolution; body-cone sensors worst (0.58). With pushes: perfect map 0.55. Training the policy with the
  planner in the loop changed nothing (S91).
- Flat-ground base rate (S92, corrected for a map-edge artefact): nominal robot falls 0 to 3 percent in 20 s; the light-ball design kb025
  and the heavy-ball kb4 carry nearly all flat falls; LQR gains cannot fix kb025 (S93). Scale study (0.75x, 1.5x, 2x robots) in progress.

## Lessons worth a page (each has a transferable rule; frame as development lessons, no blame)
1. Engine invariants: randomising body_mass without recomputing body_subtreemass corrupted the physics in proportion to distance from the
   origin (S69, S70). Rule: when batching a model field, recompute every derived field, and test far from the origin.
2. File names carry settings: results were silently overwritten until every evaluation setting went into the file name and JSON (S53 era).
3. Exposure artefacts: 8 s episodes made slow robots look safe because they never reached the obstacle (S86 withdrawn, S87). Rule: check
   that every world is exposed to the feature being measured.
4. Finite maps: robots driving off the height-field edge were counted as falls (S92). Rule: record where failures happen.
5. Reference frames for conditioning vectors: held-out designs were described relative to the wrong reference design (S85 suspended, S85-R).
6. Mechanisms need a loaded reference pose: arrestors placed at the unloaded reset pose wedged the ball (S79). Rule: settle under gravity first.
7. Smooth motion with a real sensor needs the sensor aimed where the planner is going, not where the robot is going (S90).
Not lessons (ordinary development bugs, leave out): timeouts, process-kill mistakes, misread listings, LaTeX escaping.
