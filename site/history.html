How the project got here

Log entries S0 to S138, in eight phases. Each milestone cites its entry in the study log.

S0 to S138MuJoCo CPU, then MuJoCo-Warp GPU
  • Physics first, then a little learning. Pure PPO learned to track but was slow or brittle. An LQR base with a bounded learned residual won (S60).
  • The biggest gains were not training tricks. A passive mechanism (S79), a sensor with a planner (S89), and command layers (S110, S136).
  • Corrections changed results. Each one is on the timeline in red.
Where it stands: the recommended controller is g39_goalcentre_s0LQR base + PPO residual (authority 0.25, adaptive 0.15 to 0.3), motion-reference layer, goal logic on the true ball centre, 11 designs. S121.
Timeline

Turning points, in order

Filled dots are turning points. Red is a correction, amber a limit or a null result, green a win.

  1. Phase 1 · S0 to S15

    A CPU study: make it go where it is told

    One robot, CPU MuJoCo, about 800 steps per second per run.

  2. S1correction

    The first "progress" was the robot cheating

    The policy kicked the ball out within 0.2 to 0.6 s and stood on its wheels. Results void. An off-ball check was added.

  3. S6turning point

    Capping the spin command unlocked speed

    Infeasible spin commands had been suppressing everything else. With spin capped at 1 rad/s the policy reached 0.91 of a 1 m/s command.

  4. Model, LQR

    Physics first: a derived model and an LQR baseline

    The LQR tracked at 1.00 to 1.08 of the command from 0.25 to 0.75 m/s and was 50 times smoother than PPO. It fell in every 1 m/s trial. (Log sections "First-principles model" and "LQR baseline", between S7 and S8.)

  5. S10

    First LQR + learned residual

    A zero-initialised residual at authority 0.1 kept the LQR strengths and fixed yaw. More authority (0.2) made it worse.

  6. S15limit

    Steps are a physical limit, not a learned one

    Step-trained policies were no better on 2 to 4 cm steps than flat-trained ones. Traction became the suspect.

  7. Phase 2 · S16 to S48

    Many robots, on a GPU

    The goal widened: one controller for many robot designs, terrains and disturbances.

  8. S18cnull result

    The learned LQR/PPO gate collapsed to pure LQR

    Mean gate 0.00 on the five designs checked. The "hybrid gate" idea was dropped.

  9. S21turning point

    Training moved to MuJoCo-Warp on the GPU

    About 140k steps per second with 4096 worlds: 60M steps in 7 minutes. The first transfer to the CPU engine failed because of a porting quirk, then worked.

  10. S33 to S36correction

    The first generalist looked terrible, partly by mistake

    It was far from robust (S33). Two unlearnable designs were dropped, leaving 11 (S34). Then an evaluation bug turned out to have inflated the falls (S36).

  11. Phase 3 · S50 to S68

    The structure that was missing

    Pure learning tracked speed poorly. The LQR came back as the base.

  12. S50, S53

    Pure learning was sluggish

    Slow tracking was a policy deficiency, not a physical limit.

  13. S60turning point

    LQR base + learned residual: accurate and goal-directed

    First policy with tracking error of 2 to 5 percent of the command up to 0.9 m/s and goal success 0.47. It fell more on the tiles.

  14. S61null result

    Full authority fails

    Residual authority 1.0 was worse than 0.5. The learned part has to stay bounded.

  15. Phase 4 · S69 to S78

    Two engine bugs reset the table

    The most expensive lesson of the project.

  16. S69, S70correction

    Stale subtree mass: every randomised result before this was invalid

    Mass randomisation did not update a stored engine constant, in training (S69) and then in evaluation (S70). The policies were retrained as g12. The earlier "wide randomisation does not help" and "mass is the driver" findings (S67, S68) were artefacts.

  17. S76null result

    Disturbance-focused training variants: none helped

    Goal success under pushes and pulls stayed at about 0.3.

  18. Phase 5 · S79 to S85

    A mechanical fix beats more training

    Ask why it falls before training longer.

  19. S79turning point

    The body derails off the ball; three passive arrestors stop it

    95 percent of undisturbed falls ended at the off-ball limit, with derailing at a median tilt of about 15 degrees. Motors were at their torque limit under 1 percent of the time. Arrestors at 100 degrees raised goal success from 0.57 to 0.75 with no retraining (one policy, one seed).

  20. S83turning point

    Authority 0.25 is the knee: the first "recommended controller"

    g18_ret_rs025: achieved speed 0.99 to 1.01 of the command, goal success 0.71 to 0.76 undisturbed and 0.42 to 0.43 with pushes and pulls (two seeds). Superseded later.

  21. S85-R

    Unseen robot designs

    The LQR base against pure learning on designs never trained on. Held-out designs cost more than held-out terrain (S83 addendum).

  22. Phase 6 · S86 to S109

    Finding the limits, and steering around them

    Where it still falls, and what a cheap sensor buys.

  23. S88limit

    Ball radius sets the step a ballbot can climb

    Predicted traction limit: 0.29 ball radii, 2.6 cm for the 9 cm ball (values as restated in S125).

  24. S89win

    Seeing obstacles is decisive where they can be avoided

    Behind 8 cm blocks: blind 0.09 of goals, perfect map 0.91, a small ToF sensor 0.85. Same balance controller (g18_ret_rs025_s1), 528 worlds.

  25. S91null result

    Training with the planner in the loop: a null result

    Tested and rejected.

  26. S92correction

    Flat-ground "falls" were robots driving off the map edge

    At 0.9 m/s, 97 percent of recorded flat falls were at the border. The benchmark now uses interior tiles.

  27. S98, S99correction

    Standing falls were float32 solver blow-ups

    A wheel-ball free-rolling friction of 0.001 broke the solver. It was set to 0.01 and the recipe retrained as g26_ret_rs025_mu01.

  28. S87, S100, S102correction

    Speed and steps: two reversals

    S87 said obstacle falls do not depend on approach speed. On the corrected contact model speed does matter (S100). A "family gap" in S101 was an evaluation bug and was withdrawn (S102).

  29. Phase 7 · S105 to S117

    Making it move well

    Paths oscillated and the robot hunted at the goal (S105).

  30. S106null result

    A motion layer on an unchanged policy does not help

    The policy has to be trained with it.

  31. S110 to S113turning point

    Train with a motion-reference layer and adaptive authority

    Commands are shaped before they reach the balance controller (S110). Authority is 0.15 while cruising and rises to 0.3 when manoeuvring (S112). Recipe g34 (S113).

  32. S114win

    Navigation: more goals reached, a quarter less wobble

    Same planner and sensor, new balance recipe.

  33. S115 to S117null result

    More steps, stronger pushes, longer memory: the pull limit stays

    Sustained pulls, not pushes, cause the falls in the pushed battery (S116).

  34. Phase 8 · S118 to S138

    The recommended controller, and bigger worlds

    Rebuilding the controller for ROS 2 found a bug that training had hidden.

  35. S118correction

    The goal logic followed a point that swings around the ball

    The ball body origin sits 0.14 m from the ball centre and rotates with it. Found by the ROS 2 parity work.

  36. S121turning point

    g39_goalcentre_s0 becomes the recommended controller

    Goal logic on the true ball centre. Hold error 3.6 to 4.9 cm over three seeds, against 11 to 14 cm. ToF navigation 0.87 to 0.89. It is still the recommendation.

  37. S119

    A design sweep turns into build guidelines

    One robot property changed at a time. See Design guidelines.

  38. S125, S126limit

    Steps measured properly

    The 9 cm ball passes half of the steps at 2.9 cm (g39, three seeds pooled; predicted 2.6 cm). A 0.6 m/s run-up lifts the limit from about 2.8 to about 4 cm.

  39. S127 to S133

    Bigger balls, hills, and a cost

    A stall penalty (g43, S127) and a hills tile (g44, S128) help their targets. But the ball-scaled designs in the training mix cost the heavy-body robustness that g34 had (S131, S133). g39 stays recommended.

  40. S134, S135turning point

    In a 24 m world, the goal rule is the problem, not the terrain

    On flat ground a third of the robots fell in 30 s chasing goals. Capping the commanded speed at 1.2, 0.6 and 0.4 m/s gave 0.37, 0.18 and 0.07 falls (one run per row).

  41. S136win

    Goal layer v1: far goals with a fifth of the falls

    Pursuit at a capped speed, braking, then hand-over to the trained goal rule: 0.15 falls in 60 s against 0.81. No retraining. One run per row.

  42. S137, S138null result

    Looking at the ground ahead: not better yet

    With fair starts the look-ahead layer falls 0.65 against 0.69 for the blind one. One run each.

  43. Nowin progress

    Heading command and per-feature terrain levels

    A heading layer above the policy and generated fields with a difficulty level per terrain feature are in the code (marked S139, S140). They have no log entry yet, so no result is shown here.

Engines: S0 to S20 ran on CPU MuJoCo. From S21 training and evaluation ran on MuJoCo-Warp (float32, GPU, 100 Hz). From S37 on, results use 11 designs with domain randomisation on; noise and seeds are stated in each log entry. Every number here is copied from the cited entry of docs/research/tracking_study_log.md. Simulation only: no physical robot.
