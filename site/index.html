What we built

Four pieces, one recipe

Derive the physics first, let a network correct what the model gets wrong, keep the body on the ball mechanically, and plan around what cannot be climbed.

1 · model-based

LQR on a derived model

A planar model of ball, body and wheels, linearised, with gains per design. A governor limits acceleration to 0.5 m/s2.

Q = diag(1e-6, 50, 1, 1), R = 1
2 · learned

A bounded PPO residual

A policy trained with PPO adds a correction to the LQR action, scaled by an authority of 0.15 while cruising and 0.3 when manoeuvring. It learns what the planar model leaves out.

a = clip(a_LQR + α(t) a_policy), α in [0.15, 0.3]
3 · mechanism

Three ball arrestors

In 95 percent of falls before this change the body derailed off the ball at about 15 deg of tilt. Three passive 8 mm rollers keep it on.

passive, Rezero-style
4 · navigation

A planner over a sensed map

Steps above the traction limit cannot be climbed, so a planner maps them with a forward ToF sensor and steers around. A motion layer smooths the commands and brakes into the goal.

sense, map, plan, smooth
LQR + (0.15 to 0.3) × residual + arrestors + motion layer + planner + true-centre goal

Trained on 11 robot designs with domain randomisation at scale 1.75, 200M steps. How the pieces fit: Controller.

What is honest here

Simulation only, with the failures left in

Scope

  • Everything is MuJoCo simulation. No hardware was built or tested.
  • Most rows are 1 to 3 training seeds. A fall rate over 64 worlds has a standard error up to 0.06.
  • Results from before the engine correction (S69, S70) are not used.

What failed

  • Sustained pulls: at 3 to 12 N, 36 percent fall even on flat ground. Training on stronger disturbances did not help S113, S116.
  • Steps and bumps above the traction limit, about 0.29 r, fail 90 to 98 percent at a slow approach. Seeing them does not help; a 0.6 m/s run-up lifts the limit from about 2.8 to about 4 cm (S126) S87, S88.
  • Three designs stay hard on flat ground: a light ball, a heavy body and weak motors S113 to S116.
  • Training the policy with the planner in the loop changed nothing S91.

What held up

  • The LQR base carries the policy to an unseen 1.5x robot: 0.61 to 0.71 goal success, where pure learning collapses to 0.09 to 0.15 S85-R.
  • The authority dial is a real trade-off: 0.15 gives tracking 1.01 and robust success 0.35; 0.5 gives 0.85 and 0.47 S82, S83.
  • Obstacle falls depend mostly on obstacle size; they also rise with approach speed (0.28 at 0.1 m/s to 0.51 at 0.9 m/s) S87, S100, S103.
