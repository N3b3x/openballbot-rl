Adding learning

The recommended controller is an LQR plus a small learned correction. The correction fixes what the model leaves out. It is kept small on purpose.

Simulation only, no hardware

You will be able to

  • write the rule that combines the LQR and the network;
  • explain the trade-off between tracking and robustness;
  • describe adaptive authority and how the network is trained.

4.1The residual: learn only what the LQR gets wrong

The recommended controllerg39_goalcentre_s0: LQR base + PPO residual, authority 0.25 with adaptive 0.15 to 0.3, motion-reference layer (lesson 5), goal logic on the true ball centre (lesson 9), 11 designs.study log S121

The LQR is a safe default for any robot. The network handles what the linear model ignores: friction, latency, slip, pushes, steps.

4.2Why not just learn everything?

A network trained from scratch balances well on the robots it trained on (S82). It has no physics built in, so it cannot extrapolate.

On an unseen robot 1.5 times larger, pure learning reached 9 percent of goals. Controllers built on the LQR reached 71 percent (S85-R).

4.3Authority is a dial

Low authority keeps the LQR's exact speed tracking. High authority recovers better from pushes but follows commands less well. The study mapped the curve and found a knee at 0.25 (S82, S83).

Try it

Select 0.15, then 0.5, then pure learning. Which would you choose for a robot that must follow velocity commands closely and survive being bumped? What does the single number hide?

Show the answer

0.25 is a reasonable answer: exact tracking and most of the robustness. The chart hides seed noise (the ranges are over only one or two runs), the effects on goal arrival and holding (S111: authority 0.15 fell three times as often at goals as 0.25, 0.30 against 0.11 to 0.12) and the robot design. Which is why the next step is not a single number but a rule.

So the dial now moves by itself (S112):

0.15 while cruisingexact tracking
0.3 at once when help is neededthe command changes faster than 0.3 m/s per second, the command is near zero, or the tilt passes 4 degrees
Back down over about 0.5 swhen the need has passed
The goal dead band, and a finding that was one seed

Near a goal the command goes to zero, the rule raised authority, and one training seed hunted around the goal, reversing its command 4.9 times per run. The fix (S113) is a dead band: the near-zero term only counts while the ball still moves faster than 0.05 m/s.

A later finding corrected the diagnosis: the hunting was one unlucky seed, not a property of the recipe (S113). The dead band stays because it is harmless. Training with the adaptive rule gave the lowest training fall rate of the study (0.0016, S112).

4.4How the network is trained

PPO (proximal policy optimisation) learns by trial and error. Domain randomisation makes every world a little different.

The numbers: network, observation, randomisation ranges

Every 10 ms the network sees a 25-number observation (tilt, rates, velocities, the last action, the command, the goal), the last 8 steps of history (80 ms) and a 6-number description of which robot it drives. It outputs three wheel commands. It has three hidden layers of 512 units.

Randomised: masses (15 percent), floor friction (20 percent), wheel damping (0.07 to 0.15), motor torque (15 percent), observation noise, a one-step latency (20 percent probability), and pushes of 5 to 20 N every 2 to 4 s (configs/env/dz_nom.yaml). The range is widened during training (to 1.75 times in the study recipe).

The 11 designs: the nominal one, a high and a low centre of mass, a heavy and a light ball, a heavy and a light body, a large rotor inertia, weak motors, and two wheel-contact angles.

Check yourself

The residual policy is put on a ball of a size never seen in training. Which part of the controller still does something sensible?

Why

The LQR gains come from the model of the actual robot, so they stay meaningful as designs change (S85-R: LQR-based controllers 0.61 to 0.71 on the unseen 1.5x robot). The learned part has no online adaptation and can be wrong on an unfamiliar ball: S119 found a 12 cm ball failed 69 percent with the learned controller (g34) but only 2 percent with the LQR alone. The residual limits the damage; it does not remove it.

SummaryKey takeaways

  • The residual keeps the LQR as a safe base and lets the network nudge it: applied = clip(u + rs a).
  • Pure learning does not transfer to an unseen robot (0.09 on a 1.5x robot, against 0.71 with an LQR base).
  • Authority is a dial: low is exact tracking, high is more robust to pushes. It now adapts between 0.15 and 0.3.
  • Training: 11 designs, ten kinds of terrain tile and randomised physics, 4096 worlds on one GPU, 200 million steps.
  • The recommended controller is g39_goalcentre_s0 (S121).
