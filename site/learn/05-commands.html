Lesson 5 of 1014 minCommands

Commands that feel good

A ballbot cannot follow every command a planner can write. A small layer of limits makes the commands followable, and it only works when the learned controller is trained with it.

Simulation only, no hardware

You will be able to

  • list the five things the motion-reference layer does and the limit each uses;
  • explain why abrupt commands hurt a non-minimum-phase robot;
  • say why the layer must be present during training, and which limit smooths navigation paths.

5.1The problem: a command a ballbot cannot follow

The balance controller tracks a velocity command. A planner or a goal rule produces that command, and it knows nothing about leaning. Three things go wrong when raw commands are passed straight through (S105):

  • The heading turns faster than the robot can lean into. A recorded navigation run commanded a smooth heading (1.6 rad/s RMS), but the ball's actual velocity heading swung at 8.1 rad/s RMS, with a slow sideways sway at 0.32 Hz.
  • A goal overshoot flips the command by 180 degrees. Lesson 2: reversing takes a lean in the opposite direction, which the robot cannot produce instantly.
  • Nothing holds position at the goal. The goal rule saturates, the LQR has almost no position weight, and a switch at 5 cm chatters, so the robot hunts around a goal it has missed.

The fix is a motion-reference layer between the planner and the balance controller. It turns a desired velocity into a command a ballbot can follow:

desired velocity→slew-limit speed→limit turn rate→brake before reversing→arrival braking curve→position hold→balance controller
  • Speed slew: accelerate at most 0.4 m/s2, brake at most 0.5 m/s2.
  • Turn rate: at most min(1.5 rad/s, alat / speed), with alat = 0.35 m/s2: the faster you go, the slower you may turn.
  • Reversal: a desired heading more than 90 degrees away is approached by braking first (speed scaled by the cosine of the error).
  • Arrival: near a goal the speed is capped at √(2 abrk (d − dstop)), the speed from which braking at abrk stops exactly at the goal.
  • Hold: within 6 cm and slow, a gentle position loop (gain 0.8, speed cap 0.06 m/s, dead band 1.5 cm) holds the goal.
Try it

Choose the zigzag scenario. Raise the turn-rate limit to 4 rad/s and the lateral limit to 1 m/s2. What happens to the layer's heading rate? Then choose the 180 degree turn and lower the braking limit to 0.2.

Show the answer

With the limits relaxed the layer passes most of the zigzag through and its heading rate climbs toward the raw one: the smoothing comes from the limits, not from the layer existing. In the real navigation test it was the same: raising the turn limit from 1.5 to 2 rad/s brought the path wobble back to the no-layer level (1.72 against 1.79), while accelerating and braking harder cost nothing (S114). On the turn-around, a lower braking limit makes the robot take longer to stop before it reverses, visible as a longer slow stretch in the speed plot.

5.2Does it work on the real controller?

The first test was disappointing and instructive. Putting the layer in front of an unchanged policy made goal arrival worse: falls doubled from 0.15 to 0.31 (S106). The command looked better but the policy had been trained on raw, piecewise-constant commands and had never seen slow ramps or a position-hold loop. Command shaping has to be trained with, not bolted on.

Training with the layer (S110) fixed it: goal falls dropped from 0.15 to 0.11 or 0.12, command reversals from 1.19 to 0.38 per run, hold error from 7.5 to 6.0 cm. In navigation with a planner and the ToF sensor (S114), the path wobbles a quarter less (heading-rate RMS 1.31 against 1.79 rad/s) and the layer costs only 0.3 s per route on average (median time 9.2 s against 8.9 s).

Why

That is the diagnosis in S106: the policy was trained on piecewise-constant commands and a raw goal rule. Retraining with the layer in the loop (S110) turned the same layer from a loss into a gain. The general lesson for learned controllers: the distribution of inputs at training time is part of the controller.

SummaryKey takeaways

  • Raw commands make the ballbot sway and hunt: its heading cannot turn as fast as a planner assumes, and nothing holds position.
  • The layer slews speed, limits the turn rate by lateral acceleration, brakes before reversals, brakes to goals along sqrt(2 a d) and holds position.
  • Added to an unchanged policy it doubled goal falls (S106); trained with, it improved arrival and hold (S110).
  • On navigation it cuts path wobble by about a quarter at a cost of 0.3 s per route; the turn-rate limit is the part that matters.
