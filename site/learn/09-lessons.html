Lessons from the study

Some of the most useful results of the study are corrections. Four of them, each with the habit that would have caught it.

Simulation only, no hardware

You will be able to

  • spot an exposure artefact;

9.2A: the point you measure may not be the point you think

Goal logic, goal metrics and the hold loop all used the ball body origin. In the simulator's model that origin sits 0.14 m from the ball centre and rotates with the ball.

Try it

In the toy, set the slowed speed to 0.12 m/s and move the episode length from 15 s to 45 s. At which length do the two curves meet? What would you check in any test that compares a slow controller with a fast one?

Show the answer

The curves meet when even the slowest robot has had time to reach the farthest obstacle (2.4 m at 0.12 m/s takes 20 s). The check is: did every subject meet the test? Record how many robots actually reached the feature, and compare falls per robot that did, not per robot started.

9.4C: the test map decides what the test measures

The zigzag test fell 28 to 33 percent in every recipe. It looked like a limit of the controller. But the zigzag drifted about 2 m forward, off the flat start tile and onto terrain.

On a fully flat map the same zigzag fell 5 percent (S113). The claim was withdrawn.

9.5D: one seed is not a result

Several early conclusions fell to this rule: the goal hunting behind the dead band (S113), the hard-design weighting gain (S114), the benefit of tracking weight 3 (S112, S113).

Check yourself

Recipe B scores 0.78 against recipe A's 0.76 on one seed each, and B is cheaper. What should you conclude?

Why

In the data above, seeds of one recipe differ by 0.01 to 0.02 in this metric, as much as the gap. The study logs single-seed results as such and retracts them when a later seed disagrees.

SummaryKey takeaways

  • Check what a measurement is attached to: the ball body origin swung 14 cm around the true centre (S118).
  • Count the subjects that met the test (S103).
  • The test map is part of the test: the zigzag falls were terrain, not turning (S113).
  • A single seed is not a result: compare differences with the seed spread.
  • Keep a ledger of corrections.
