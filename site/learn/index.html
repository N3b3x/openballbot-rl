How a ballbot works, and how this one was built

A tall robot balancing on a single ball is unstable, and the way it moves starts in the wrong direction. Ten lessons take you from that physics through the controller, the sensing, the design rules and the ROS 2 deployment of this project, with simulations that run in your browser and a question after each idea.

How the course works

Each lesson has learning goals, a short explanation, at least one interactive figure, a question with an answer reveal, key takeaways, and links to the code and the study-log entries behind every number. Figures load only when you scroll to them, work with the keyboard, and stop animating if your system asks for reduced motion.

You need high-school physics and a feeling for graphs. Calculus and matrices help but are not required: where an equation appears, a plain-words reading comes with it.

The thread: a physical model (lessons 1 to 3), a controller that adds learning without giving up the physics (4 and 5), what it needs to sense and know (6 and 7), what the robot should look like (8), how to trust the numbers (9), and how it leaves the simulator (10).

Honest limits. Everything here is MuJoCo or MuJoCo-Warp simulation with domain randomisation. Where a figure is a simplified stand-in (a lag instead of the balance controller, a toy for exposure), its caption says so.

planar model→LQR→+ PPO residual→motion layer→planner + ToF→odometry→ROS 2 bundle
